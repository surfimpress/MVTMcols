Haiku vs Sonnet comparison — Almonte Gazette, 1899-09-22, page 6

Page picked from the DB for a genuine mix of body text and registered ads (7 columns, 3 with ads, 4 plain text). Each column sent to both models in parallel; neither result was ingested to the production DB — this is exploratory only.

What actually differed

  • Haiku's JSON envelope broke 4 of 7 times (57%); Sonnet's broke 0 of 7. In every broken case the cause was the same: Haiku used a straight " or ' inside dialogue/ possessives instead of the required curly “”‘’, which prematurely closed the JSON string. The transcripts shown below for the affected Haiku columns were recovered with a best-effort quote-repair heuristic for display purposes — in production these would have needed a retry.
  • Sonnet flagged repair_needed on all 7 columns; Haiku flagged it on only 2 of 7. Every Sonnet repair reason checked out as a real, specific finding (unregistered ads, blank masked rectangles not in the registered list) — not a vague or boilerplate flag. Haiku appears to skip the "cross-check registered ads against what's visible" step from its instructions most of the time.
  • Diplomatic fidelity: Sonnet preserved a printed typo with [sic] ("pay he[sic] highest prices"); Haiku silently normalized the same line to "the highest prices" — exactly the kind of silent correction the agent instructions prohibit.
  • Transcript length: Sonnet's transcripts ran consistently longer (5–15% more characters per column on the longer columns), consistent with catching additional live ad text Haiku left out.
  • Speed: Haiku averaged ~107s per column, Sonnet ~183s (about 1.7x faster) — the one dimension where Haiku clearly wins.
